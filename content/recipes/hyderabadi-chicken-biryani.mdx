Biryani rewards patience more than skill — a good marinade and a proper dum seal will get you further than a fancy technique. This is the two-pot method: chicken cooked most of the way through, rice parboiled separately, then layered and finished together under a sealed lid.

## Method

### Marinate

1. Mix the chicken with yogurt, half the ginger-garlic paste, half the biryani masala, chilli powder and salt. Marinate for at least 2 hours, ideally overnight in the fridge.

### Cook the chicken

2. Heat ghee in a heavy pot. Add the whole spices and let them sizzle for a few seconds.
3. Add the remaining ginger-garlic paste and green chillies, cook for a minute.
4. Add the marinated chicken and cook on medium-high heat until it's about 80% done and the gravy has thickened — 12–15 minutes. Stir in half the fried onions, mint and coriander.

### Parboil the rice

5. Soak the rice for 30 minutes, then boil in salted water with a whole spice or two until it's 70% cooked — it should still have bite in the centre. Drain immediately.

### Layer and dum

6. Spread the chicken and its gravy evenly across the base of the pot. Layer the parboiled rice on top.
7. Scatter the remaining fried onions, mint, coriander and the saffron milk over the rice. Drizzle with a little ghee.
8. Seal the pot tightly — a dough seal around the lid works best, but a tight-fitting lid with a damp cloth underneath does the job too.
9. Cook on very low heat (use a tawa underneath if your stove runs hot) for 25–30 minutes. Don't open the lid early.
10. Rest for 10 minutes off the heat, then open and fold gently from the bottom up before serving, so every plate gets both layers.

Serve with raita and a simple onion salad — biryani doesn't need much else.
