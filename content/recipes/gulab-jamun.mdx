The two things people get wrong with gulab jamun are frying them too hot and rushing the soak. Low, slow oil gets you a jamun that's cooked through and not raw in the centre; a proper rest in warm syrup is what makes them burst when you bite in.

## Method

### Make the syrup

1. Combine sugar, water and crushed cardamom in a pan. Bring to a simmer and cook for 8–10 minutes until it's a one-string consistency. Stir in the rose water and keep the syrup warm.

### Make the dough

2. Knead the khoya, paneer, maida and baking soda together into a smooth, crack-free dough, adding milk a teaspoon at a time only if it feels dry. Don't overwork it.
3. Divide into small portions and roll into smooth balls with no cracks — cracks are where the syrup gets in unevenly and the jamun falls apart.

### Fry and soak

4. Heat the ghee or oil to medium-low — test with a small piece of dough; it should rise slowly without browning immediately.
5. Fry the balls in batches, stirring gently and constantly so they colour evenly, until deep golden brown all over. This takes longer than you'd expect — 8–10 minutes a batch.
6. Drain and slide directly into the warm syrup. Let them soak for at least 2 hours before serving, so they plump up properly.

Serve warm or at room temperature, syrup and all.
