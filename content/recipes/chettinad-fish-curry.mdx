This is the curry I make when I want something with a bit of a temper. Chettinad food doesn't hide behind cream or cashew — the heat and the roasted spice are the whole point. Fresh fish takes to it well because the gravy is doing most of the talking.

## Method

1. Heat the oil in a heavy-bottomed pan. Add mustard seeds and let them splutter, then add the curry leaves.
2. Add the chopped onion and sauté on medium heat until it turns a deep golden brown — don't rush this part, it's where the base flavour comes from.
3. Stir in the ginger-garlic paste and cook until the raw smell disappears, about 2 minutes.
4. Add the chopped tomatoes, turmeric and Chettinad masala. Cook down until the oil starts to separate at the edges of the pan.
5. Add the grated coconut and cook for another 3–4 minutes, stirring so it doesn't catch.
6. Strain in the tamarind water, add a cup of water, and bring the gravy to a simmer. Salt to taste.
7. Slide in the fish pieces gently — don't stir too much or they'll break apart. Cover and simmer for 10–12 minutes, until the fish is cooked through and the gravy has thickened slightly.
8. Rest for 5 minutes off the heat before serving — like most curries, it tastes better once it's had a moment to settle.

Serve with plain rice or with dosa the next morning, if there's any left.
