This isn't a quick pickle — it's the kind you make once a year when raw mangoes are in season and it sees you through to the next. The mustard oil needs to be heated and cooled before it goes in, or the pickle turns bitter, and the mango needs to be bone dry before it's cut or it won't keep.

## Method

1. Wash the mangoes and dry them completely with a cloth — any moisture left on the skin will spoil the pickle. Dice into bite-size pieces with the skin on.
2. Toss the mango with salt and turmeric in a large, completely dry bowl. Set aside for 2–3 hours; it'll release some liquid.
3. Heat the mustard oil until it just starts to smoke, then take it off the heat and let it cool down to room temperature — this mellows the oil's raw sharpness.
4. Dry-roast the fenugreek seeds lightly until fragrant, then grind coarsely along with the mustard seeds.
5. Mix the mango with the ground spices, chilli powder and asafoetida. Pour over the cooled mustard oil and mix well, making sure every piece is coated.
6. Pack into a completely dry, sterilised glass jar, pressing down so the oil rises to cover the mango.
7. Leave the jar in direct sunlight for 4–5 days, giving it a stir with a dry spoon once a day. The mango will soften and the flavours will come together.

Once it's cured, keep it in a cool, dry place — always use a dry spoon, and it'll keep for months.
